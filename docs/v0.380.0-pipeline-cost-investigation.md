# v0.380.0 — Why a consumer sprint burns hours before any code, measured

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Question:** the reference consumer spent ~19h on a two-item sprint (one bug fix, one percentage
selector reusing an existing component) without reaching implementation. Why, and why have repeated
attempts at it produced only tactical fixes?

**Method:** four parallel hands against the consumer's own recorded artifacts, read-only. Eleven
hypotheses were raised and eleven were refuted on measurement. This document records the
refutations, because the expensive failure mode here is re-deriving them.

**Standing:** the conclusions below are measurements over `s288–s304` of the reference consumer.
Every count is a floor and every correlation is over n≤14 sprints. Where a figure could not be
established the section says so rather than estimating.

---

## 1. What is actually true

**Planning wall clock is uncorrelated with sprint scope and tracks process volume.**

| correlate | Pearson r |
|---|---|
| stories delivered | 0.060 |
| product LOC changed | 0.049 |
| **planning artifact count** | **0.953** |

The discriminating pair: **s297 shipped 11 stories, 49 acceptance criteria and 6,508 lines of
product code on 2.41h of planning. s301 shipped 0 stories and 450 lines on 22.67h.**

**Convergence depth roughly tripled at a dated boundary.** Six sprints at mean depth 1.20–2.60, then
four at 3.33–6.00; the ranges do not overlap. The only large consecutive delta is **s297→s299**,
window `2026-07-24T13:07Z → 2026-07-27T15:23Z`. Step size **+2.99 passes** on the capital-path-matched
set.

**The pipeline is lead-bound.** Lead-serial time is 5.15h / 3.69h / 2.30h for s302 / s303 / s304
against an agent critical path of 3.05h / 2.97h / 2.93h — the lead binds in two of three sprints
before any scheduling change is considered.

**Nothing measures any of this.** `Measured` appears 240 times in `CHANGELOG.md` against **one**
instance of post-release efficacy phrasing. The tripling ran seven sprints without being noticed.

## 2. The eleven refutations — do not re-run these

1. **Consumer instruction surface.** `SKILL.md` + `steps/` + `team-roles/` grew 176,584 → 702,224
   bytes (3.98×) between v0.1.0 and v0.379.0, monotone. Real, but it is a **lead** cost and the lead
   is 27% of live time. Not the driver.
2. **Artifact size drives depth.** r=0.105, indistinguishable from the scope control (0.152), and
   measured with the bias in its favour — size was taken at series end, after repairs had grown it.
   No threshold exists either: early-era depth is flat at ~2.1 across a 16× size range, and every
   threshold step tested is within noise or negative. This also answers the question left open when
   the artifact budgets were set: there is no convergence-cost threshold to derive.
3. **The rulebook moving under a series.** **0 of 8** adversarial series spanned a version change.
   The operator pulls between sprints, not during: s303 held one version across a 98-hour planning
   phase. The early era was the *noisier* one (mean churn 0.33, depth 1.98) against the late era
   (0.00, depth 4.73).
4. **`validation_intensity`.** Null. s296, s297, s298 and s299 are all `standard`; s297 = 1.60,
   s299 = 6.00. The step happens entirely inside one intensity.
5. **A stricter adversary.** `core/team-roles/adversary.md` **shrank** across the boundary
   (21,441 → 21,321 bytes, 13 insertions / 12 deletions, all editorial). The severity ladder, the
   Rule 8 pass floors and the `EXIT_CONDITION_MET` predicate are unchanged; the one substantive edit
   loosens.
6. **The five new spec-layer gate checks.** `core/skills/ai-dlc/steps/gate-validation.md` grew 23%
   and its check count went 32 → 37 in the window — but depth rose **less** on the subjects those
   checks govern (+1.58) than on the ungoverned ones (+2.18), and `prd`, governed by three of the
   five, went **down** 0.50.
7. **The cross-artifact byte-identity join.** **81% of the finding-rate rise occurs in passes that
   never mention `locked-requirements.md`** (93% excluding one outlier). Within-subject the effect is
   +0.43 with signs ++--. The subject with the highest late finding rate cites the kernel least.
8. **Superlinearity in corpus size.** r=−0.051 over 38 consecutive-pass pairs. On the cleanest
   series, per-pass duration *fell* 11.5m → 7.9m while the corpus grew 36%.
9. **Concurrency across artifacts.** An agent-only critical path suggested 2.9–3.3×. The DAG charged
   the lead nothing. With lead cost charged the honest figure is **1.17–1.62×**, and **0.87–1.07×**
   if one `DIVERGENT_HARD_BLOCK` lands — two of three sprints finish *later* than they actually did.
   Both are upper bounds. Killed. Separately, the dependencies are real: `Check 3b` joins the first
   artifact to the last at byte level, and a *MINOR* repair in s292 broke byte-identity between
   `prd.md` and `product-brief.md`, costing a full divergence cycle with the brief already frozen.
10. **"Catch mechanical findings before the adversary."** Already done and largely spent. Mechanical
    share of pass-2+ findings: s302 65% → s303 83% → **s304 29%**; the counterfactual saves 40% /
    45% / **9%** of passes.
11. **"Require the remediator to fence counts it changes."** The duty already exists in
    `core/team-roles/remediator.md` and is followed: 69 repair records carry 487 `derivation:` fields
    and **693** fenced derivation blocks. The prose shape this was aimed at appears **once** in three
    sprints.

## 3. The surviving explanation, and why it is probably not testable

The opus tier changed inside the boundary window — the last `claude-opus-4-8` record is
`2026-07-24T19:04:16Z` and the first `claude-opus-5` is `2026-07-24T20:38:33Z`. On the **22 gate
checks whose text is byte-identical either side of the boundary**, the failure rate went
**2.09% → 7.16%**.

**Read that dating with its caveat.** Those two instants come from the `model` field of the
consumer's `subagent-context.jsonl`, and §4 establishes that the field records the **lead's** model,
not the teammate's — it agrees with the teammate's true arm on 41.2% of rows against 89.7% for the
dispatch guard's `model_bound`. So the instants date the **lead session's** arm. A tier rollout is a
platform change and almost certainly moved teammates at the same time, but that is an inference from
the timing rather than a measurement of it, and it is not established here. The gate-check figure is
independent of that field and stands on its own.

**This does not cancel out against the authors also improving**, for three reasons:

- **The roles are pinned to different tiers.** From `tier_pinned` in the spawn ledger, written by
  `core/hooks/ai-dlc-dispatch-guard.sh` at PreToolUse: adversary **opus** (156), remediator **opus**
  (138), gate-adjudicator **opus** (78); analyst **sonnet** (76), dev **sonnet** (58), pm **sonnet**
  (30), qa **sonnet** (26). `architect` is opus, so the split is not clean.

  **CORRECTED — the pin-versus-run claim that stood here is REFUTED, and its refutation makes this
  reason stronger rather than weaker.** This paragraph read *"131 of 395 adversary spawns ran sonnet
  despite an opus pin ... a third of the largest reviewer population is not opus"*. Re-derived by
  resolving each named spawn-ledger row to the teammate's own transcript and taking that
  transcript's last assistant `model`: the pin agrees with the arm actually run on **603 of 707**
  resolvable rows (85.3%), against a control with the arm flipped at 0.7%. But **99 of the 104
  disagreements are rows whose `tier_pinned` is NULL** — no pin was recorded, so nothing diverged.
  True pin-versus-run divergence is **4 rows in 707**. For the adversary specifically it is **ZERO**:
  140 adversary spawns resolve to a transcript, all 140 opus-pinned, all 140 ran opus. Control in the
  same invocation: the ledger holds 156 adversary rows, every one opus-pinned, and the 16 unresolved
  are unresolved because their transcripts were reaped. A denominator of 395 is not constructible
  from that ledger at all. So writers-sonnet / reviewers-opus holds, `architect` is opus, and the
  split is imperfect only in that direction — no argument here needs the caveat that was written.
- **The exit test reads the reviewer only** — `findings_critical == 0 && findings_major == 0`. A
  better author moves where the artifact *starts*; a better reviewer moves where it must *finish*.
  Only one of the two is in the termination condition.
- **One rung fires on the absence of evidence.** `core/team-roles/adversary.md` makes an underived
  factual claim a MAJOR "whether or not you can yet falsify it", while the derivation grammar in
  `core/scripts/validate-artifact-derivations.sh` is opt-in for the author. Reviewer improvement is
  fully expressed against that rung; author improvement is capped by a convention the author may
  decline.

The observed signature matches: **findings per pass-2+ pass fell 39% while the number of passes rose
63%.** The loop got longer, not richer.

**Whether the extra passes bought anything is not answerable from this corpus.** Every quality signal
it holds — gate verdicts, escalations, adversarial findings — is produced by the models whose
capability changed. The only non-model-generated signal is fix-forward commits, and there are five in
the entire window.

## 4. Instrument defects found along the way

Each of these produced, or nearly produced, an inverted conclusion.

- **Every transcript-derived field in the consumer's `subagent-context.jsonl` was the LEAD's, not the
  teammate's.** `.transcript_path` at SubagentStop is the lead's transcript and both read sites take
  from it, so `peak_tokens`, `turns`, `compactions`, `model`, `end_ts`, `duration_s` and the `role`
  fallback were all the lead's. Three independent proofs: the implied starts (`ts − duration_s`) for
  one sprint collapse onto three values matching that sprint's three harness sessions, 48 rows sharing
  one minute, against a control of 118 distinct `ts` minutes over the same 145 rows; nine stop-seconds
  carry more than one record and **eight of the nine are byte-identical** on
  `peak_tokens|turns|compactions|model` across distinct agents (control: `agent_id` distinct in 9 of
  9); and the probe's `model` matches the lead's own recorded arm on **95.9%** of rows (1248 of 1301;
  control, lead arm flipped: 4.1%). **That figure was published here as 99.1% and does not
  reproduce** — re-derived by joining each probe row to the on-disk teammate transcript, taking the
  parent session file as the lead and comparing last-assistant `model` on each side. The conclusion
  is unchanged and the margin against the 41.2% teammate agreement below is what carries it. Measured against teammates' own transcripts, eight rows, every field wrong:
  peak over-reported 25–160%, `model` wrong on 7 of 8, `compactions` reporting 1 where the truth is 0,
  duration inflated 16–40×.

  **This voided the instrument's own purpose.** The hook exists to answer "how close do teammates get
  to the threshold, and do any of them compact". Its recorded peak maximum exceeds the threshold it is
  read against — that is the lead crossing its own ceiling — while true teammate peaks sit well below
  it and true `compactions` is zero. **The `autoCompactWindow` question it was built for was argued
  from lead data.** A wrong `model` reading has also been cited as the entire evidence of an operator
  escalation, in which the ledger row for the *remediation* of the disputed dispatch reads identically
  to the violation.

  **It never worked** — the earliest day in the record already reads 100% of runs over 900s. Fixed in
  this release by repointing both reads at the teammate's own transcript, one level down at
  `<slug>/<session-uuid>/subagents/agent-<agent_id>.jsonl`; see
  `core/hooks/ai-dlc-subagent-probe.sh`.

- **The header calibration was a sound measurement orphaned onto the wrong column.** Its figures
  (1,979 runs, p50 4.1m / p90 15.0m / p99 61.6m / max 699m) come from a pre-implementation-time study
  in the same release that added the field — one of three independent estimators, measured by a
  different method — and were attached to a column that did not exist when they were taken. Nothing
  ever compared the two. The figures are the right order of magnitude for real teammate runtime, which
  is why they read as confirmation for a month.

- **The fixture could not fire on any of this, and never could.** It handed the hook a
  teammate-shaped transcript directly — a layout that does not occur in production — so its
  `duration_s` assertion certified exactly the assumption the hook violated. Reshaped in this release
  to build the real two-file layout, and proven in both directions: the fixed hook passes 26 of 26,
  the real pre-fix hook fails 14 field assertions.
- **A text classifier tuned on one era does not transfer.** 86% agreement and 0.94 recall on
  late-era prose; **0.33 recall** on early-era prose. Shipped, it would have reported "27% → 75%
  mechanical, the lever is real". The hand classification says 79% → 69% — the opposite direction.
- **A composite token set can invert an adoption date.** `LR-S<n>` and `LOCKED_REQUIREMENTS` predate
  the spec layer by eleven sprints; the locked block lived inside `product-brief.md` with the same id
  convention. Only the filename token has a clean 0% early-era rate. The composite set reported 76%
  early-era citation of an artifact that did not exist.
- **A bare grep for `DIVERGENT_HARD_BLOCK` over-reports by 8–9×**, because most hits are records
  stating that it does *not* apply. Anchor on the emitted `^verdict:` stamp.
- **Substring collisions mis-bucketed whole pipeline steps.** `research` contains `arch`;
  `brainstorm` contains `stor`. Classify dispatches by time window, not by name.
- **A full-sprint window manufactures a correlation.** Version churn reads r=+0.506 measured over the
  whole sprint and **−0.241** per day; r(window length, depth) = 0.873 was the real content.
- **A missing node silently deletes a DAG edge.** One artifact in two sprints had no
  `gate-adjudicator` dispatch, so a modelled constraint vanished and the schedule read 3.84h instead
  of 6.08h — the same shape as a check that cannot fire.

## 5. What this release ships, and what it does not

**Ships:** the two defects in `core/hooks/ai-dlc-subagent-probe.sh` — `duration_s` measured from the
dispatch instant recorded in the spawn ledger, and the `|| echo 0` conflation that turned a failed
read into a measured zero (`BL-073`). Both stale calibrations are removed from that file's header;
they were not merely out of date but unreproducible from any point in the recorded corpus, and they
read as confirmation while the field beneath them emitted session age.

**Does not ship, and is deliberately not proposed:** any reduction in validation intensity. The
intensity dial is the operator's, `full` is chosen deliberately for capital-path code, and
recommending `standard` is a scope reduction rather than an efficiency change. Concurrency across
planning artifacts is refuted above and should not be revisited without new evidence about the
lead's serial cost.

**Left open:** the pipeline still has no model-independent quality signal, so the question in §3
remains unanswerable. One such number recorded per sprint — a test-suite result, or a defect-escape
count taken at retro — would make it answerable next time.
